Debt snowball vs avalanche: which should you use?

Both methods work the same way: pay the minimum on every debt, then put every extra dollar on one target debt. The only difference is which debt you target first.

SnowballAvalanche
Target firstSmallest balanceHighest interest rate
Main benefitQuick wins that keep you motivatedUsually the least interest overall
Best ifYou need early progress to stick with itYou're motivated by saving the most money

An example

Say you owe $4,200 on a card at 24.99%, $1,800 on a card at 19.99%, and $9,500 on a car loan at 6.9%, and you can pay $535 a month in total.

  • Avalanche clears the 24.99% card first. Everything is paid off in about 35 months, with roughly $2,990 in interest.
  • Snowball clears the $1,800 card first, in about 14 months. Everything is paid off in about 35 months, with roughly $3,130 in interest.

Here avalanche saves about $140 in interest, while snowball gives you your first payoff more than 10 months sooner. Your own numbers will differ, so run them in the calculator.

How to choose

If the interest difference is small, pick snowball for the motivation. If it's large, pick avalanche. The best method is the one you'll keep doing every month.

Make either method easier to stick with

  • Set up autopay for every minimum so nothing is late.
  • Write your debt-free date somewhere you'll see it.
  • Track progress visually. Shading one block for every $100 paid makes progress easy to see.
